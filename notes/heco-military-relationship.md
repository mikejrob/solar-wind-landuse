# Hawaiian Electric and the U.S. military's electricity systems on Oahu

Date: 2026-07-28. Neutral reference. Answers the repo owner's question — HECO
"has some kind of agreement to manage their electricity systems" — and asks how
that relationship bears on the military's inclination toward solar. Reports
REVEALED CONDUCT and STRUCTURE only. Draws no conclusion about HECO's motive or
intent; the rate-base-interest question stays OPEN. Cross-links
`notes/military-land-solar.md` §6e (which this note verifies and deepens).

## Headline (what the relationship IS)

The "agreement to manage their electricity systems" is a **U.S. Army Utilities
Privatization (UP) contract**, awarded to Hawaiian Electric by the Defense
Logistics Agency (DLA Energy) in 2019. Under it HECO **owns, operates,
maintains, and upgrades the on-base electric distribution system — the poles and
wires inside the fence — at 12 Army installations on Oahu** for a **50-year
term** valued at **$638.5 million**. This is not a supply-to-the-fence-line
arrangement: before the contract the Army distributed power internally on base
and billed its own users; under the contract HECO took over ownership and direct
customer service of the in-base distribution grid (VERIFIED, Tier 1).

The UP contract is a distinct thing from three other, separate HECO–military
relationships, which should not be conflated:
1. **How the military buys bulk power** — HECO tariff Schedule DS (large-power
   directly served), plus standard retail.
2. **HECO-owned generation physically on military land, in HECO's general rate
   base** — the ~20 MW West Loch PV array and the 50 MW Schofield biofuel plant.
3. **IPP generation on military land that HECO does NOT own** — Kupono Solar
   (Navy EUL, Ameresco/Bright Canyon), sold to HECO under a PPA.

The UP distribution contract is billed to the Army SEPARATELY and is walled off
from HECO's general rate base by PUC condition (see below) — a nuance §6e of the
land note did not draw out. Items (2) ARE in the general rate base; the UP
distribution assets are not.

## 1. The utilities-privatization / management agreement (scope, sources)

**The authority.** DoD Utilities Privatization runs on **10 U.S.C. § 2688**
(utility-system conveyance authority; 1997). It lets a service secretary
**convey ownership** of an installation's utility distribution system to a
private/municipal/cooperative utility, transferring "operations, maintenance,
repair, future upgrades, and future systems replacements." The same authority
line permits a utility-services contract term of **up to 50 years** where a
business-case analysis shows it cost-effective
([10 U.S.C. § 2688, uscode.house.gov](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title10-section2688&num=0&edition=prelim);
[GAO-20-104, DoD Utilities Privatization](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-20-104.pdf)).
(Tier 1.)

**The award.** DLA Energy solicitation **SPE600-16-R-0809**, "Privatization of
the Electric Distribution System at U.S. Army Garrison-Hawaii, Island of Oahu,"
competitively bid from 2016; awarded to Hawaiian Electric **October 3, 2019**;
value **$638.5M**, **50-year** term
([Star-Advertiser, "HECO wins $638.5M Army contract," 2019-10-04](https://www.staradvertiser.com/2019/10/04/hawaii-news/heco-wins-638-5m-army-contract/);
[Hawaii Public Radio, 2019-10-03](https://www.hawaiipublicradio.org/local-news/2019-10-03/heco-wins-639m-50-year-contract-as-army-privatizes-its-electric-system);
[Army Technology, 2019](https://www.army-technology.com/news/dla-hawaiian-electric-us-army-system/);
solicitation record via govtribe SPE600-16-R-0809). HEI reported the award in an
SEC Form 8-K dated 2019-09-27
([HEI 8-K exhibit, SEC EDGAR](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/354707/000035470719000107/exhibit998-k092719.htm) —
filing exists; body returned 403 to the fetcher, so the 8-K text itself is
UNVERIFIED-by-direct-read, but the award facts are Tier 1 from the news record).
(Tier 1 for award facts.)

**Scope — own-the-wires, confirmed.** HECO "will own, operate and maintain the
electric distribution system serving the 12 installations," performs
Army-identified capital improvements, and replaces aging distribution
infrastructure ([Army Technology](https://www.army-technology.com/news/dla-hawaiian-electric-us-army-system/)).
The own-vs-supply distinction resolves toward OWN: HPR reports that "presently,
HECO provides electricity and the Army distributes it internally, billing users,"
and under the new arrangement "HECO assumes ownership and direct customer service
responsibilities"
([HPR](https://www.hawaiipublicradio.org/local-news/2019-10-03/heco-wins-639m-50-year-contract-as-army-privatizes-its-electric-system)).
So HECO holds the in-fence poles-and-wires, not merely a meter at the boundary.
(Tier 1.)

**The 12 installations** (Oahu, Army): Schofield Barracks Main Post, Schofield
Barracks East Range, Wheeler Army Airfield, Fort Shafter, Tripler Army Medical
Center, Fort DeRussy, Helemano Military Reservation, Aliamanu Military
Reservation, Pililaau Army Recreation Center, Kahuku Training Area, Dillingham
Military Reservation, Makua Valley Training Area
([Daily Energy Insider, PUC greenlight](https://dailyenergyinsider.com/featured/27795-hawaiian-puc-greenlights-hawaiian-electric-acquisition-of-electric-systems-for-12-u-s-army-facilities/)).
(Tier 1/2 — installation list is from trade press; a DLA/PUC filing would
firm it. Note overlap with the training/ordnance parcels in the land note §1–2:
several of these are the very ranges excluded there for solar.)

**PUC approval and the ratepayer wall.** Filed as **PUC Docket No. 2019-0349**;
the Commission **approved on ~November 1, 2020** (order number not captured — a
docket pull is needed, flagged UNVERIFIED). HECO **took ownership and full O&M
on March 1, 2022** after a one-year transition
([Daily Energy Insider](https://dailyenergyinsider.com/featured/27795-hawaiian-puc-greenlights-hawaiian-electric-acquisition-of-electric-systems-for-12-u-s-army-facilities/);
army-technology, above). Critically, the PUC ordered that HECO **"cannot shift
any expenses from the Army system onto general ratepayers,"** and costs "deemed
unrecoverable from the Army" cannot be recovered from general ratepayers; the
Army pays through a **special billing separate from its regular monthly
electricity bill**, with reported figures of **$16.3M in monthly bill credits**
and **$24.4M of upgrade expense recouped over five years**
([Star-Advertiser, "State regulator OKs...," 2020-11-03](https://www.staradvertiser.com/2020/11/03/hawaii-news/state-regulator-oks-hawaiian-electrics-takeover-of-army-power-service-on-oahu/)).
(Tier 1 for the ratepayer-separation condition; the two dollar figures Tier 2,
single-source, worth a docket confirm.)

STRUCTURE (labeled, not motive): a UP contract is a regulated revenue stream —
HECO earns a return on the capital it invests in the Army distribution system and
recovers O&M from the Army — but it sits OUTSIDE the general rate base by PUC
condition. So the land note's §6e framing ("adds to HECO's rate base") is
imprecise for the UP contract specifically; it is a separately-billed Army
obligation, not a general-ratepayer rate-base asset. The rate-base point holds
for the generation in §3, not for the distribution contract.

## 2. How the military buys power (tariffs)

**Schedule DS — Large Power Directly Served Service** (the large-customer tariff
the military-scale loads take): **applicable to loads ≥ 300 kW, supplied and
metered at a single voltage and delivery point and served directly from a
substation.** Customer charge $425/mo, demand charge $23.00/kW-mo, non-fuel
energy 1.8886 ¢/kWh, plus all standard surcharge clauses (ECRC, PPAC, RBA, IRP,
PBF, REIP, Green Infrastructure). Authority: **Docket No. 2016-0328, Order No.
35721, filed 2018-09-28**, sheet effective 2019-01-01
([HECO Schedule DS tariff PDF](https://www.hawaiianelectric.com/Documents/my_account/rates/hawaiian_electric_rates/heco_rates_sch_ds.pdf),
extracted locally). (Tier 1.)

**CORRECTION to `military-land-solar.md` §6e:** that note says Schedule DS
"requir[es] a contract of at least five years." The tariff sheet as read states
**no minimum contract term** — its threshold is the 300 kW / single-substation
service condition, not a 5-year commitment. The "five years" claim is
**UNVERIFIED and appears incorrect**; it may have been conflated with the UP
contract's cost-recovery period or a different rate rider. Recommend striking or
sourcing it. (Flagged.)

**Standby / backup and self-supply tariffs** — NOT verified for federal
customers. Whether a base with behind-the-meter generation would take a HECO
standby/backup rate, and whether net-energy-metering or a self-supply/CSS tariff
is available to a federal customer served on HECO-owned on-base distribution, is
**UNVERIFIED** and material to the solar question (§4). Needs a tariff/docket
review. (Tier 3 gap.)

## 3. HECO-owned military-serving generation (in the general rate base)

- **Schofield Generating Station** — **50 MW** dispatchable biofuel/diesel (six
  Wärtsilä engines), on an **8-acre Army lease** at Schofield Barracks,
  **owned and operated by HECO**, online 2018; "feeds into the island's
  electrical grid and serve[s] all customers on Oahu"
  ([Star-Advertiser, 2018-06-01](https://www.staradvertiser.com/2018/06/01/business/heco-army-complete-schofield-generating-station/);
  [Utility Dive, 2014](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/heco-to-add-50-mw-fast-acting-power-plant-to-support-solar-integration/326221/)).
  Rate-base cost-recovery via a Major Project Interim Recovery (MPIR)
  mechanism: **Order No. 35953, Docket No. 2017-0213**, effective 2018-10-01
  ([HECO Adequacy-of-Supply / RSOP filings, puc.hawaii.gov](https://puc.hawaii.gov/wp-content/uploads/2021/02/GEN-RPT.HECO_.ADEQUACY-OF-SUPPLY-2021.pdf)).
  Firm generation, NOT solar — but the clearest black-start/islanding asset on
  Oahu (land note §6b). (Tier 1 for ownership/size/lease; Tier 2 for the exact
  MPIR docket string.)
- **West Loch PV** — **~20 MW-AC**, on ~100 ac at the Navy's West Loch Annex,
  **HECO builds, owns, and operates** it under a Navy lease; described by HECO as
  the second-largest PV in the state at signing
  ([Star-Advertiser, 2016-07-21](https://www.staradvertiser.com/2016/07/21/breaking-news/navy-heco-team-up-for-100-acre-solar-facility-at-west-loch-annex/)).
  RAM/RSOP filings reference a "West Loch PV" scenario in HECO's revenue
  treatment ([HECO RSOP transmittals, puc.hawaii.gov](https://puc.hawaii.gov/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/05-21-2019-HECO-Companies-RSOP-Revised-Transmittals-Book-2-Trans-19-01-02-03.pdf)).
  (Tier 1 for HECO ownership; exact rate-base treatment Tier 2.)
- **Contrast — Kupono Solar** (Navy West Loch EUL, 42 MW PV + 42 MW/168 MWh
  storage, Ameresco + Bright Canyon JV, 20-yr HECO PPA, COD Jun 2024): HECO
  does **NOT** own it; it is IPP generation HECO buys under contract (land note
  §3). The two West Loch arrays sit side by side under opposite ownership
  models — HECO rate-base (~20 MW) vs IPP-PPA (42 MW). (Tier 1.)

STRUCTURE (labeled): HECO earns a regulated return on the ~20 MW West Loch array
and the 50 MW Schofield plant because they are utility-owned and in the general
rate base. An IPP self-build or a DoD-owned microgrid on the same land yields
HECO a PPA margin or nothing, not a rate-base return. This is a structural
incentive gradient, stated as structure. It is NOT evidence that HECO chose
ownership to capture it, and no document examined states such intent.

## 4. Bearing on military solar — open questions (revealed conduct + structure)

The load-bearing new fact for the solar question: **as of March 2022 HECO owns
the distribution grid inside 12 Army installations.** Anything the Army wants to
connect behind its own meter on those bases now interconnects to **HECO-owned**
wires and is metered by HECO. What follows is STRUCTURE and OPEN QUESTIONS, not a
finding.

- **Interconnection now runs through HECO end-to-end on Army bases.** A base
  adding its own PV/microgrid must interconnect to a HECO-owned distribution
  system under HECO's interconnection rules and (possibly) standby/backup
  charges. Whether that is friction-laden or routine is **UNVERIFIED** and is
  exactly the kind of question a PUC-docket / interconnection-agreement review
  would resolve. (Tier 3 gap.)
- **Structural incentive gradient (labeled structure).** A utility earns a
  regulated return on rate-base distribution and rate-base generation; it earns
  a contract margin on a PPA; it earns nothing on a customer's behind-the-meter
  self-build that reduces billed load. On the Army bases, HECO now holds BOTH
  the distribution return (UP contract) AND, at two sites, the generation return
  (§3). This is a structural map of who earns what, not a claim about conduct.
- **Countervailing structure — the ratepayer wall and separate billing.** The
  UP distribution business is walled from general ratepayers and billed to the
  Army separately (§1). And DoD's own resilience mandate (10 U.S.C. §§ 2920,
  2911; land note §6a) pushes the Army TOWARD on-base solar-plus-storage and
  islanding regardless of HECO's position. Both cut against a simple
  "HECO blocks base solar" story. Kupono itself — a large IPP solar+storage
  islandable microgrid on military land, not HECO-owned — is a REVEALED
  counter-example: the incumbent's ownership of nearby distribution/generation
  did not prevent an IPP self-supply project next door (on Navy, not the
  UP-covered Army, land — note the jurisdictional difference). (Tier 1 for the
  Kupono fact; the inference is offered as structure.)
- **No documented HECO–military dispute found.** No instance was found in this
  pass of HECO and the military contesting on-base generation, interconnection,
  islanding, or standby rates in any proceeding. Absence of found conflict is
  NOT evidence of absence — the UP interconnection terms and any base-generation
  interconnection studies are not in the public record reviewed. **UNVERIFIED.**

3. HECO **standby/backup and self-supply/NEM tariff** availability to a federal

4. Any **interconnection agreements or PUC filings** for Army-side generation
