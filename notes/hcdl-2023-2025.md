# The USDA/UH Hawaiʻi Cropland Data Layer (2023–2025) against the HDOA 2020 baseline on Oʻahu

Analysis run: 2026-09-07. Script: `analysis/hcdl_overlay.py`. Outputs:
`data/oahu_hcdl_class_acres.csv`, `data/oahu_hcdl_by_soil.csv`,
`data/oahu_hcdl_vs_hdoa2020.csv`, `data/oahu_hcdl_stability.csv`,
`data/oahu_parcel_hcdl.csv`, `data/oahu_hcdl_change_candidates.csv`,
`data/oahu_hcdl_verification_sample.csv`, `data/oahu_existing_solar_hcdl.csv`.
Warped rasters: `data/gis/hcdl/hcdl_{2023,2024,2025}_oahu_26904.tif`
(gitignored, regenerable).

## Finding

The two products agree on where Oʻahu's cropland is and disagree, by
construction, on how much of it is planted in a given year. HCDL maps
18,968 ac of crop cover in the ag district in 2024 (19,057 in 2023;
19,804 in 2025) against HDOA's 21,227 ac of cropped footprint in 2020.
On B/C soils the two are within 6% (HCDL 2024: 10,039 ac; HDOA 2020:
10,637 ac). Of HDOA's 2020 cropland, 60% is HCDL crop in all three years,
19% is crop in some years, and 21% is never crop in 2023–25. The
never-crop share is mostly seed-corn and pineapple rotation ground that
HDOA counts as part of the operation and HCDL classes as grassland in
years it is not planted. Pasture cannot be compared: HDOA's fenced
commercial pasture (14,912 ac) is 38% HCDL grassland and 59% forest,
shrub, or other non-agricultural cover. Every built solar farm reads as
"Developed" in HCDL, so crop-to-developed transitions are a usable
signal for new solar and housing; 619 ac of HDOA 2020 cropland is
developed or barren in all three HCDL years, on 7 parcels that match
known projects (Mililani solar, West Loch, Hoʻopili, Whitmore). The
remaining differences (about 2,500 ac of crop-to-grassland and 1,100 ac
of unmapped-to-crop) are candidates, not changes, until checked against
imagery. A verification sample of 28 parcels is in
`data/oahu_hcdl_verification_sample.csv`.

## The product

USDA NASS and UH Mānoa (Qi Chen, Geography; Noa Lincoln, TPSS) produce
the Hawaiʻi Cropland Data Layer (HCDL): an annual 10 m raster of 27
classes (21 agricultural, 6 non-agricultural). Version 2.1 for 2023, 2024,
and 2025 was released 2026-06-12
(https://www.nass.usda.gov/Research_and_Science/Cropland/Release/datasets/Hawaii_CDL_2023_2024_2025.zip;
cached `data/raw/hcdl/`, with metadata, the V2.0 summary, and Li et al.,
IGARSS 2026). Method: per-pixel Random Forest on Google DeepMind's
AlphaEarth Foundations 64-dimensional annual satellite embeddings, trained
on UH Mānoa ground-reference data collected December 2023 – October 2024
on Kauaʻi, Oʻahu, Maui, Molokaʻi, and Hawaiʻi Island. The 2023 layer was
produced from 2023 embeddings with the 2024-trained model, since 2023
ground data was thin (V2.0 summary, p. 1). No accuracy assessment has
been run for 2025 (2025 metadata). The 2024 statewide F1 scores from the
metadata (validation set 11,681 samples, IGARSS paper §IV.A):

| class (CDL code) | F1 | class | F1 |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| Corn (1) | 0.965 | Pineapple (98) | 0.998 |
| Misc Vegs & Fruits (47) | 0.985 | Taro (100) | 0.985 |
| Other Crops (106) | 0.901 | Banana (93) | 0.974 |
| Other Exotic Fruits (94) | 0.922 | Papaya (97) | 0.975 |
| Coffee (95) | 0.950 | Coconut (102) | 0.899 |
| Grassland/Pasture (176) | 0.920 | Developed (82) | 0.935 |
| Forest (63) | 0.953 | Shrubland (152) | 0.936 |

Macro-average F1 0.965. Non-agricultural classes inherit the National
Land Cover Database and NASS says to use NLCD for those. "Other Crops"
is the catch-all for crops without their own class (and on Oʻahu is the
largest crop class at 9,858 ac in the district), and has the lowest crop
F1. Rasters are EPSG:3857 at a nominal 10 m (about 9.3 m on the ground at
21.5°N; 86.5 m² per pixel); the script warps them by nearest neighbour
onto the repository's 10 m EPSG:26904 grid so every cell is 100 m² and
aligns with the slope, LSB, district, and parcel masks.

## How the two products differ by design

| | HDOA 2020 baseline | HCDL 2023–25 |
|---|---|---|
| unit | digitized field polygons, commercial operations ≥3 ac | 10 m pixel |
| what is counted | land deployed to an operation: roads, sheds, buffers, rotation ground, freshly tilled fields | cover classified in that year's embeddings |
| seed corn | gross seed-company footprint (~25% planted at a time) | corn where corn was growing |
| pineapple, papaya | active production areas; fallow rotation excluded for papaya | planted cover |
| pasture | fenced commercial cattle land | NLCD-style grassland cover |
| accuracy | landowner review, site visits; no statistic | F1 by class; 2025 unassessed |
| update | 5-year, manual | annual |

So HCDL crop acreage should sit below HDOA's for gross-footprint crops,
HCDL grassland should not be read as pasture, and the closest legitimate
comparison is HCDL crop cover against HDOA cropped categories, on the ag
district and by soil class.

## Existing solar farms as HCDL sees them

`data/oahu_existing_solar_hcdl.csv` (OSM footprints ≥10 ac). Built farms
are "Developed" in every year: Kawailoa 212 of 284 ac, Waipio 181 of 245,
West Loch 82 of 101, Lanikuhana 83 of 106, Eurus Waiʻanae 141 of 143;
the rest of each footprint is grassland (the margins and between-row
ground). Waiawa Phase 2 (271 ac, pasture in HDOA 2020) is still grassland
through 2025. New solar on cropland therefore shows as crop → developed,
and on pasture as grassland → developed.

## Candidate changes

Parcels where HDOA 2020 and all three HCDL years disagree by ≥5 ac
(`data/oahu_hcdl_change_candidates.csv`; 135 parcels, 5,214 ac):

| type | parcels | acres | 2024 composition of the disagreeing cells |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| HDOA crop, HCDL non-crop all 3 years | 88 | 4,093 | grassland 2,525; forest/shrub 1,000; developed/barren 619 |
| HDOA unmapped, HCDL crop all 3 years | 47 | 1,121 | mostly "Other Crops" |

The largest crop-to-grassland cases are seed-corn ground in Kunia and
Waialua (Bayer, Corteva) and pineapple ground on Kamehameha Schools and
Dole land: consistent with rotation, not loss. The crop-to-developed
cases are 7 parcels, 571 ac: Bayer Kunia (28 ac), Dole Wahiawā (73 ac),
Cedar Grove Hawaii at Whitmore (70 ac), Navy West Loch (54 ac, the West
Loch solar site), Villa Rose Waialua (33 ac, egg-farm structures), D.R.
Horton Hoʻopili (26 ac), Mililani I Land Holdings (23 ac, Mililani
solar). One owner attribution is suspect: TMK 192005022 (1,508 ac of
HDOA crop, 681 ac now grassland) resolves to "Island Palm Communities"
(Army housing); UNVERIFIED, check RPAD.

## Are the differences real? A verification plan

The differences have three sources, and only the third is change over
time: (1) definition (gross footprint vs annual cover; pasture vs
grassland); (2) classification error (HCDL F1 0.90–0.97 for the relevant
classes, 2025 unassessed, 2023 model-transferred; HDOA digitization
error, unquantified); (3) real conversion, abandonment, or new planting
2020→2023–25. The plan:

1. Treat 3-year-consistent disagreement as the candidate set (done;
   135 parcels). One-year disagreements are dropped as rotation or noise.
2. Stratify and check against imagery. `data/oahu_hcdl_verification_sample.csv`
   holds 28 parcels with centroid coordinates and Google Earth links,
   `verification_status` = UNVERIFIED, and blank columns for the checker:
   A. crop → developed/barren (7 parcels, 571 ac): confirm against the
      project census (`data/hawaii_solar_project_census.csv`), building
      permits, and dated imagery; expect these to be real.
   B. crop → grassland (9 largest, 2,041 ac): view 2020, 2023, 2024, 2025
      imagery; if bare or planted in any year, it is rotation and the HDOA
      footprint definition explains the difference; if grass in every
      year since 2020, it is abandonment or a change of operation.
   C. unmapped → crop (12 largest, 777 ac): "Other Crops" commission is
      the risk (F1 0.90); confirm crop rows, orchards, or greenhouses in
      imagery; check whether HDOA excluded them under the 3-ac or
      non-commercial rules rather than missed them.
3. Add the HDOA 2015 layer (geodata `LandUseLandCover/MapServer/4`) as a
   third time point; a parcel cropped in 2015 and 2020 and never in
   2023–25 is a stronger abandonment signal than 2020 alone.
4. Ask the producers (Zhe Li, NASS; Qi Chen, UH Mānoa) for the Oʻahu
   validation points and the confidence layer, which is not in the
   public release. Per-class user's and producer's accuracy on Oʻahu
   would let the candidate acreage carry an error band.
5. Report any confirmed change with its imagery date, and keep
   unconfirmed differences labelled as such in the paper.

## Caveats

- HCDL grassland is not pasture. Do not update the pasture figures from
  HCDL.
- Warping to the UTM grid resamples 86.5 m² pixels to 100 m² cells; area
  totals are within 1% of the source raster's Oʻahu tallies.
- The crosswalk assigns Other Crops, Misc Vegs, Sweet Basil, Sweet
  Potatoes, and Sunflower to "diversified"; Coconut, Citrus, Avocado, and
  Other Exotic Fruits to "tropical fruits". HDOA folds small plantings of
  banana, papaya, and fruit into Diversified Crop, so HCDL's specific
  fruit classes exceed HDOA's.
- Class 0 (background) is ocean and off-island; 473,656 ac of the
  grid.
