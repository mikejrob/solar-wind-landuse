# Official land screens: how HECO and HSEO estimate Oʻahu land for utility solar

Every official estimate of Oʻahu utility-solar land since 2021 rests on one
screen: NREL's PV-Alt-1, prepared for Hawaiian Electric (3,810 MW on about
24,700 acres). HECO's Integrated Grid Plan, its Renewable Energy Zone study,
HSEO's 2023 decarbonization pathways report, and HSEO's 2026 fuels study all
adopt it without re-deriving it. The screen excludes all state and federal
land, the Urban district, and 90% of class B/C soils, and counts class D/E
in full on slopes to 30%. Built Oʻahu utility solar sits mostly on the land
the screen excludes (`notes/ag-land-use-baseline.md`, "Existing solar
farms"). HECO's plan of record (the Land-Constrained Preferred Plan) assumes
land is limited "due to current land use policies" and names no future
solar beyond the procurement pipeline. HSEO's 2026 review of the
Hartley/Roberts report calls land "often the central constraint" and offers
no screen of its own.

Compiled 2026-10-07 for the UHERO blog draft
(`paper/UHERO-blog-Oahu-land-solar-DRAFT.md`). Quotes were checked against
text extracted from each PDF. Page numbers are printed pages unless marked
"PDF p."

## 1. The common screen: NREL PV-Alt-1 [V]

Grue, Waechter, Williams & Lockshin, *Assessment of Wind and Photovoltaic
Technical Potential for the Hawaiian Electric Company*, NREL, 2020, updated
2021-07-30.
https://www.hawaiianelectric.com/documents/clean_energy_hawaii/integrated_grid_planning/stakeholder_engagement/stakeholder_council/20210730_sc_heco_tech_potential_final_report.pdf

| Item | Value | Location |
|---|---|---|
| PV-Alt-1, Oʻahu, 1-axis tracking | 3,810 MW | Table ES-2, p. v; Table 29, p. 83 |
| PV-Alt-2 (adds DoD land) | 7,026 MW | same tables |
| PV-Alt-3 (slope ≤15%) | 1,405 MW | same tables |
| PV-Alt-4 | 2,932 MW | same tables |
| Original (non-Alt) Oʻahu scenarios | 561 MW (PV-3-3) to 13,965 MW (PV-2-HS) | Table 5 and summary table |
| Capacity density | 38 MW/km² (0.154 MW/acre, about 6.5 acres/MW) | Appendix D, p. 81 |

PV-Alt-1 exclusions (Table 28, p. 82):

- Excluded outright: DoD, National Guard, NPS, USFWS, and other federal
  lands; every state-land category, including "Other State Lands";
  wetlands; Flood Zone A; tsunami evacuation zones; 3.2 ft sea-level-rise
  zone; Important Agricultural Lands; urban areas and all Oʻahu LUO urban
  zones; golf courses; HCDA affordable-housing projects.
- Soils: "Class A Land: exclude"; "Class B Land: 10% Inc"; "Class C Land:
  10% Inc". Classes D and E carry no exclusion row.
- Slope: included to 15%; 15–30% included with a 5¢/W cost adder (p. 81);
  above 30% excluded.
- No parcel-size minimum and no distance-to-transmission screen.

## 2. Hawaiian Electric [V]

**Integrated Grid Planning Report** (2023), PUC Docket 2018-0165.
https://hawaiipowered.com/igpreport (local copy:
`~/Research/oahu-grid/data/raw/grid/heco_igp_report_2023.pdf`).

- p. 42 (PDF p. 54): "On O‘ahu we evaluated an additional pathway called
  'Land-Constrained' to represent the possibility that there would be
  insufficient land to site large-scale renewable energy projects."
- Table 3-1, p. 42: the pathway "recognizes the possibility on O‘ahu that
  insufficient land may be available to develop large-scale resources or to
  produce local biofuels … while balancing other State goals of affordable
  housing and food sustainability."
- Table 6-16, p. 86 (PDF p. 98): every scenario uses "NREL Alt-1" except
  Land-Constrained, which uses "Land-Constrained Resource Potential."
- Table 6-19, p. 92 (PDF p. 104): "O‘ahu 3,810 [MW] 24,711 [acres]". The
  listed exclusions include "Soil ratings of Class A and 90% of Class B and C
  land," IAL, federal/DoD land, urban zones, and tsunami evacuation zones.
- p. 92: stakeholders "raised concerns on the ease of developing projects
  at slopes higher than 10% … other stakeholders thought that solar on
  higher slopes could be developed, up to 30%."
- p. 127 (PDF p. 139): the Technical Advisory Panel "noted that this
  scenario does not meet our goal of 70% carbon reduction by 2030 and that
  the assumptions in this scenario to constrain the available large-scale
  renewables may be closer to reality than other scenarios."

**IGP Supplemental Response**, 2023-11-14, Docket 2018-0165 (local copy:
`~/Research/oahu-electricity-v1-corrected/sources/repair_time_research/heco_filings/IGP-supplemental-response-20231114.pdf`;
public URL not recorded).

- p. 7 (PDF p. 10): "The Land-Constrained Preferred Plan represents the
  least-cost plan in a scenario where the potential for land-based
  resources is limited due to current land use policies and the balancing
  of competing state goals for affordable housing, food sustainability and
  renewable energy."
- p. 7: the Base plan "assumes that land for energy projects on Oʻahu
  becomes more available than it is today." Hawaiian Electric "proposes
  that the Land-Constrained Plan be the Preferred Plan for Oʻahu through
  2035."
- p. 10 (PDF p. 13): "because the future development of solar was limited
  to the land-constrained scenario, no further future solar resources are
  named." Grid-scale solar in this plan equals the procurement pipeline
  (`notes/process-cost-channel.md`).

**PUC modifications to IGP Inputs & Assumptions**, letter of 2022-03-31,
p. 10 (local copy:
`~/Research/oahu-electricity-v1-corrected/sources/repair_time_research/heco_filings/IGP-inputs-assumptions-20220331-with-mods.pdf`).
Oʻahu REZ solar potential by slope: 958 MW at ≤15% slope (RESOLVE groups
A 84 + B 439 + C 435) and 2,386 MW above 15% (426 + 1,235 + 725), 3,344 MW
in total. Seventy-one percent of the screened potential is on slopes above
15%. p. 9: the PUC requires a cost adder for all utility-scale solar "in
excess of the maximum installable capacity determined for the PV-Alt-3
scenario."

**Transmission REZ Study** (Nov 2021), Table 3-1: Oʻahu utility solar
potential 3,344 MW, "adjusted from the NREL Alt-1 scenario to subtract the
capacity of existing or approved solar projects." REZ workshop deck,
https://www.hawaiianelectric.com/a/10175, p. 12: REZ groups are "identified
per NREL PV MW potential (Alt-1 scenario)."

## 3. Hawaiʻi State Energy Office [V]

**Hawaiʻi Pathways to Decarbonization** (Act 238 report, Dec 2023).
https://energy.hawaii.gov/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/Act-238_HSEO_Decarbonization_FinalReport_2023.pdf

- p. 204 (PDF p. 205): technically feasible land is "defined in the Alt-1
  scenario of the 2021 NREL … study. Land use, or capacity density values,
  were set to 0.154 MW/acre for solar PV."
- Table 28, p. 205: Oʻahu feasible solar land 24,700 acres.
- p. 206: Oʻahu "is the only island with capacity expansion results that
  approach the maximum land area available."

**Alternative Fuel, Repowering, and Energy Transition Study** (revised May
2026).
https://energy.hawaii.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/HSEO-Alternative-Fuels-Study-Revised-May-2026-w-Appendices.pdf

- p. 6 (PDF p. 7): Oʻahu has "limited land availability."
- pp. 6–7: "Assuming, 0.15 MW / acre, HSEO estimates the installed capacity
  of this solar [the IGP's ~3,300 MW] will require approximately 22,000
  acres of land, occupying approximately 90% of the technically feasible
  land." Cites Grue et al.
- Engage appendix, PDF p. 211: the study uses "the Alt-1 land exclusions."
- The study's Oʻahu reference case builds about 915 MW of utility solar by
  2045, about a quarter of the screen (Hartley & Roberts, *Hawaiʻi's
  Electricity Future*, pre-v1.032, §2.6 and §4.5,
  https://github.com/mikejrob/oahu-electricity-future/blob/main/report/DRAFT_v7_full.md).

**2025 Annual Report.**
https://energy.hawaii.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/2025-HSEO-Annual-Report.pdf

- PDF p. 3: "On Oʻahu, where population density and land constraints make
  the transition more challenging…"
- PDF p. 11: "Land availability and other factors indicate that local
  energy supply is insufficient…"
- No acreage or screen of its own.

**Issue brief: Review of the Hartley/Roberts Report**, 2026-07-08.
https://energy.hawaii.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Hartley_Roberts-Report_HSEO-Review_7-8-26.pdf
(archived: `sources/official-land-screens/hseo-review-hartley-roberts-2026-07-08.pdf`)

- p. 2: the report's "centerpiece assumption of installing 5,243 MW of
  utility-scale" solar "on 31,500 acres on Oʻahu is deeply flawed …
  Screened acreage is not the same as developable acreage."
- p. 3: "Land use is not a minor implementation detail … it is often the
  central constraint." Cites 357 MW of existing utility solar and the
  cancelled Paeahu project (Maui).
- Offers no alternative acreage or screen; asks whether the report's
  figures are MWdc or MWac (p. 3).

**FGE, *Economics of Accelerating Hawaiʻi's Energy Transition via LNG***
(Aug 2024, prepared for HSEO).
https://energy.hawaii.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/5-FGE-LNG-and-Hawaiis-Energy-Transition-_-Final-Resource-Deck.pdf
PDF p. 12, in a Hawaiʻi Gas section: electricity storage "has a large
footprint for a space-constrained island such as O‘ahu." No land
quantification in the deck.

## 4. Screen versus built record

| PV-Alt-1 rule | Built Oʻahu utility solar (OSM footprints >20 ac, 2026-10-06) |
|---|---|
| All state and federal land excluded | Kupono (42 MW) is on Navy land at West Loch (unmapped in OSM). |
| Urban district excluded | 679 of 1,697 ac (40%) is Urban district: Waiawa 1 and 2, Hoʻohana, Kalaeloa Solar Two, most of Eurus Waiʻanae. |
| Class B/C counted at 10% | 675 ac (40%) is ag-district B/C, mostly through special use permits (`notes/sup-census.md`). |
| Class D/E counted in full, slopes to 30% | 342 ac (20%) is ag-district D/E. |
| 6.5 acres/MW | The repository and the companion report use 5 acres/MW-AC (LBNL, Bolinger & Bolinger 2022, via the companion report §2.5). |

Footprint shares: `notes/ag-land-use-baseline.md`, "Existing solar farms",
computed from `data/gis/osm_solar_plants_oahu.json` (Overpass pull
2026-10-06). The companion report reaches the same result by capacity: at
least 80% of built utility-scale capacity sits off class D/E (§2.5).

The repository's own screen (`notes/available-land-map.md`) is built the
same way as PV-Alt-1 (class A out, B/C at 10%, graduated slopes to 30%,
military out) and lands within 10% of NREL's footprint (companion report
§2.6). The difference between the official conclusions and the companion
report's lies in use of the screen: HSEO prices utility solar with a larger
Hawaiʻi premium than offshore wind and builds a quarter of the screen;
HECO's plan of record names no solar beyond the pipeline.
