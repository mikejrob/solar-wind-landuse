# Why so little grid-scale solar? The utility-process-friction hypothesis

Oahu has built little utility-scale solar, and the documentary record locates
the chokepoint in the HECO-administered procurement and interconnection process
rather than in land supply or community opposition. Whether the utility uses
that process deliberately to slow independent grid-scale solar is a working
hypothesis (Mike Roberts, 2026-07-24): "the utility has been skillful at using
bureaucracy to kill it; that's an easy tool for a utility to lever in Hawaii."
This is an INTENT claim. The record shows the revealed conduct — a slow,
risk-shifting, utility-run process — but does not by itself establish intent.
State it as a hypothesis, marshal the conduct for and against, and let a null
result stand if that is where the evidence lands.

## The revealed conduct (verified elsewhere in the repo)

Where Oahu utility-scale solar dies, by stage:

- **Not land-use.** The Land Use Commission approved every solar special use
  permit it received (8 dockets, 7 approved, 0 denied, 0 intervenors, median
  ~6 months) ([[sup-census]]). Land-use permitting kills nothing
  ([[project-pipeline-mortality]]).
- **Not community resistance.** Community resistance was the operative cause of
  death for 0 Oahu utility-scale solar projects across a 60-project census; it
  killed 1 Maui solar, 2 wind, 1 biomass ([[hawaii-project-census]],
  [[community-resistance-cases]]).
- **At procurement and interconnection.** Oahu solar dies inside the staged RFP
  (PUC Docket 2017-0352, HECO-administered) and the interconnection process;
  median award-to-COD is ~4.5-5.5 years; cancellation causes are supply chain,
  developer credit, interconnection cost and delay, and PPA price rigidity
  ([[heco-solar-procurement]], [[project-pipeline-mortality]]).

The process HECO administers shifts risk toward the independent developer:

- PPA terms — availability lump-sum rather than energy payment, zero-degradation
  assumptions, 98% availability, full interconnection-cost allocation, and a
  self-build asymmetry — most of them softenable by PUC order but not softened
  ([[heco-ppa-terms]]).
- The gen-tie interconnection path is a chokepoint: Rule 14H studies, and an IPP
  cannot condemn an easement across a neighbor's land the way a public utility
  can (HRS 101-4) ([[neighbor-opposition-and-interconnection]]).
- The friction is priced: award prices roughly doubled (Stage 1 ~$0.08-0.10/kWh
  to Stage 3 ~$0.21-0.23), realized deployment runs ~25-45 MW/yr against the
  ~200 MW/yr a 2045 build needs, and as-of-right sub-5-MW parcels fall below the
  RFP's 5 MW floor with no route to a PPA ([[process-cost-channel]]).

## The contrast that keeps the hypothesis live

Distributed rooftop solar, where the utility controls less of the process
(customer-sited, net-metering / customer-grid-supply tariffs rather than a
utility RFP), reached roughly 800 MW on Oahu — among the highest per-capita
penetration in the United States — while the utility-scale IPP fleet stayed
small (rooftop figure from the companion `~/Research/oahu-grid` modeling inputs;
tier: plausible, confirm exact MW against HECO/DBEDT). The same sun, the same
island, the same grid: the segment the utility procures moved slowly; the
segment it does not procure moved fast. This is a testable revealed-conduct
contrast, not proof of intent, and it is the strongest single reason to keep the
hypothesis open.

## Structure versus intent

- **Structure is documented.** A rate-of-return utility earns on rate-base
  capital, so it has a standing incentive to prefer utility-owned generation and
  storage over IPP power it only buys (Averch-Johnson; CLAUDE.md hypothesis 2).
  HECO owns generation in its rate base (e.g., the Schofield plant, the West
  Loch array; [[military-land-solar]]) and can self-build under the RFP. This is
  an incentive that exists whatever any individual intends.
- **Intent is not observable** from the documents gathered. The record shows
  friction and risk-shifting terms; it does not show a decision to use them to
  kill solar. Per the repo's evidentiary rule, do not assert the motive — report
  the conduct and let readers weigh it.

## Counter-evidence (report honestly)

- Much procurement delay is exogenous: pandemic-era supply chain, tariffs,
  developer credit, and global inflation drove documented cancellations that no
  utility manufactured ([[heco-solar-procurement]]).
- HECO's procurement is under PUC oversight with an Independent Observer (Bates
  White); it is not unilateral.
- HECO has filed pro-solar testimony in SUP dockets and public statements
  ([[sup-census]]); its public posture is not anti-solar.
- The land caps — the other big constraint — were requested by the
  agricultural-preservation coalition, not the utility, which stayed silent in
  the Chapter 205 bills ([[sb631-2011]], [[acts-2014-2022]]). The utility's role
  concentrates in the PROCESS layer, not the land-statute layer. Keep those two
  obstacles distinct.

## What would move this from hypothesis toward finding

- Reconcile the rooftop-vs-utility-scale MW contrast against primary HECO/DBEDT
  data, and normalize by what each segment could have supplied.
- Read the PUC dockets for patterns in HECO's use of self-build rights,
  interconnection-study timelines, and PPA-term positions across the Stage 1-3
  RFPs — revealed conduct, comparable across projects.
- Benchmark HECO's award-to-COD and PPA terms against KIUC and mainland
  procurers already assembled in [[procurement-comparison-kiuc-ercot]] and
  [[heco-ppa-terms]]: process design, not intent, is what those comparisons can
  attribute.

## Tiering

- VERIFIED: the stage-of-death finding (procurement/interconnection, not land or
  community), the PPA-term asymmetries, the price/deployment channel — all
  sourced in the linked notes.
- PLAUSIBLE: the rooftop-vs-utility-scale contrast (exact rooftop MW to confirm);
  the rate-base structural incentive (documented as structure, not as exercised
  intent).
- UNVERIFIED / not established: that HECO deliberately uses process friction to
  kill independent grid-scale solar. This is the hypothesis, not a finding. A
  null result — the friction is structural and exogenous, not weaponized — is a
  publishable outcome and must be reported if that is what the dockets show.
