Description:
Abstract:
The USDA National Agricultural Statistics Service (NASS) Cropland Data Layer (CDL) is an annual raster, geo-referenced, crop-specific land cover data layer produced for the COntinental United States (CONUS) using satellite imagery and extensive agricultural ground reference data. The Hawaii Cropland Data Layer (HCDL) is the first-ever raster, geo-referenced, crop-specific land cover data layer for Hawaii at a 10-meter spatial resolution for the years 2023, 2024 and 2025. Both years of data were released to the public August 25, 2025, on the official download website: <
https://www.nass.usda.gov/Research_and_Science/Cropland/Release/index.php
>. Please note that no farmer reported data are derivable from the Cropland Data Layer. This paper contains more information: Li, Z., Chen, Q., Yang, Z., Chen, H., Lincoln, N.K., Cao, C., Mueller, R., and Willis, P., 2024, Innovative Crop Mapping in Hawaii: The First 10-Meter Resolution Cropland Data Layers for 2023, The 12th International Conference on Agro-Geoinformatics. Novi Sad, Serbia, 2024, pp. 1-6, 10.1109/Agro-Geoinformatics262780.2024.10660811.
The HCDL leveraged the 64-dimensional geospatial embeddings from the Satellite Embedding dataset developed by Google DeepMind's new geospatial AI model, AlphaEarth Foundations. This dataset was derived from multiple datastreams, including optical, radar, LiDAR, and other sources. Ground-reference data for crop type classification was developed based on satellite/aerial imagery interpretation and field surveys conducted by the University of Hawaii at Manoa beginning December 2023. Fieldwork covered the islands of Kauai, Oahu, Maui, Molokai, and the Big Island. HCDL 2025 was derived from 2025 embeddings using the SmileRandomForest classifier in Google Earth Engine, based on the 2025 ground-reference data. Since the embedding space is consistent across years, it can be used for condition change detection. Due to the limited ground- reference data available in 2023, HCDL 2023 was derived from 2023 embeddings solely based on the trained 2024 models, thus reducing uncertainties caused by the subjective human-involved training data labeling process. More information available in this paper: Li, Z., Chen, Q., Yang, Z., Mueller, R., Willis, P., Chen, H., Lincoln, N.K., and Cao, C., 2026, Hawaii Cropland Data Layers (HCDL) v2.1: A Satellite Embedding Approach to Crop Mapping, IGARSS 2026 - 2026 IEEE International Geoscience and Remote Sensing Symposium, Washington D.C., United States, August 9 - 14, 2026.
HCDL was a collaborative effort between USDA NASS and the University of Hawaii at Manoa. This research is part of AgriWatch: Innovating Agricultural Disaster Response with AI-Empowered Real- Time Monitoring project supported by the USDA National Institute of Food and Agriculture (NIFA) Agriculture and Food Research Initiative Competitive Grants Program under award number [2024- 68016-41561].

Purpose:
The purpose of the Cropland Data Layer Program is to use satellite imagery to (1) provide supplemental acreage estimates to the Agricultural Statistics Board for the state's major commodities and (2) produce digital, crop-specific, categorized geo-referenced output products.

Attribute_Accuracy_Explanation:
The strength and emphasis of the CDL is crop-specific land cover categories. The accuracy of the CDL non-agricultural land cover classes is entirely dependent upon the USGS, National Land Cover Database. Thus, the USDA NASS recommends that users consider the NLCD for studies involving non-agricultural land cover.
An F-1 score provides a single measure of classification accuracy by combining how often the model correctly identifies a class and how reliably those identified results are correct. Higher F-1 scores indicate stronger overall performance for that category. This metric helps users quickly assess the quality of the classification without needing to interpret precision and recall separately.

Process_Description:
OVERVIEW: The HawaiiCropland Data Layer (HCDL) is the first-ever raster, geo-referenced, crop-specific land cover data layer for Hawaii. Unlike the 10-meter resolution CDL for CONUS or HCDL V1.x, which utilized 10-day image composites of surface reflectance data across multiple spectral bands and NDVI, crop classification for HCDL 2023, 2024 and 2025 V2.1 leveraged the 64-dimensional geospatial embeddings from the Satellite Embedding dataset developed by Google DeepMind's new geospatial AI model, AlphaEarth Foundations. This dataset was derived from multiple datastreams, including optical, radar, LiDAR, and other sources. Ground-reference data for crop type classification was developed based on satellite/aerial imagery interpretation and field surveys conducted by the University of Hawaii at Manoa beginning December 2023. Fieldwork covered the islands of Kauai, Oahu, Maui, Molokai, and the Big Island. HCDL 2025 was derived from 2025 embeddings using the SmileRandomForest classifier in Google Earth Engine, based on the 2025 ground-reference data. Since the embedding space is consistent across years, it can be used for condition change detection. Due to the limited groundreference data available in 2023, HCDL 2023 was derived from 2023 embeddings solely based on the trained 2024 models, thus reducing uncertainties caused by the subjective human-involved training data labeling process. Novi Sad, Serbia, 2024, pp. 1-6, 10.1109/Agro-Geoinformatics262780.2024.10660811.

Entity_and_Attribute_Information:
Overview_Description:
Entity_and_Attribute_Overview:
The strength and emphasis of the CDL is crop-specific land cover categories. The accuracy of the CDL non-agricultural land cover classes are entirely dependent upon the NLCD. Thus, the USDA NASS recommends that users consider the NLCD for studies involving non-agricultural land cover. The Hawaii CDL aims to map major, specialty, and culturally significant crops in Hawaii. The "Other Exotic Fruits" category encompasses a variety of tropical fruits such as mango, litchi, longan, cacao, dragon fruit, jackfruit, breadfruit, and rambutan. The " Misc Vegs & Fruits " category includes tomato, pumpkin, turmeric, mustard, eggplant and others.
